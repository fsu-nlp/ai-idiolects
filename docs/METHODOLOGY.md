# Methodology

This documents how the corpora in this study were assembled, cleaned, and measured. Commands
for reproducing each step are in [`ANALYSES.md`](ANALYSES.md); the model roster
and per-provider settings are in [`MODELS.md`](MODELS.md).

## Design

The study compares two cohorts of six models each, generated on identical
prompts, to ask whether individual LLMs carry stable and distinguishable
linguistic profiles and whether those profiles shifted between cohorts.

- **2024 cohort** — six English streams taken from the TextualLLMap deposit
  (Improta, Veltri & Stella 2024).
- **2026 cohort** — six models generated for this study.
- **Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 appears in both**, as a 2024-cohort member via the
  deposit and as a 2026 re-run. It is the bridge between the cohorts and the
  methodological link to the source study.

Counting the two Mistral replication runs separately, the analyses cover
fourteen sub-corpora: twelve models plus two replication runs.

Every corpus spans the same four topics (`climate`, `global_warming`,
`math_anxiety`, `misinfo_health`) at 1,000 texts per model per topic.

## Corpora

### 2024 cohort

Fetched by [`../scripts/generate/download_data.py`](../scripts/generate/download_data.py)
from [`osf.io/uwv59`](https://osf.io/uwv59) on 2026-03-24: six English streams,
24,000 texts. The deposit also holds five Italian streams, which this study
does not use.

The deposit contains more than its accompanying paper describes. Two of the six
English streams — GPT-4o and a 70B Llama — are not documented there, and no
generation parameters are published for them anywhere. See
[README § Data provenance](../README.md#data-provenance) and
[`../KNOWN_ISSUES.md`](../KNOWN_ISSUES.md), which also records two inherited
model-labelling problems that this repository deliberately does not paper over.

### 2026 cohort

Generated by [`../scripts/generate/generate_texts.py`](../scripts/generate/generate_texts.py)
against OpenAI-compatible endpoints. A single code path covers every model (local servers, self-hosted vLLM, and three hosted APIs) with only `--api-base`,
`--api-key` and `--model-id` changing. No script touches a GPU directly.

Roster selection was constrained deliberately, and those constraints bound what
the comparison can show:

- **Non-reasoning variants throughout.** Long chain-of-thought output makes
  text-level fingerprinting noisy and incomparable against non-thinking peers.
- **Open weights capped at roughly 15B**, served at FP16 on one instance, so
  quantisation does not become a second source of variation.
- **Cost-efficient tiers for the closed APIs** — the tiers
  high-volume production systems actually run.

### Human reference data

Two English corpora provide an outer reference point: Webis-CMV-20 (Reddit
r/ChangeMyView) for cross-author comparison and Reuters-50/C50 for within-author
calibration. Neither is prompt-matched to the generation task, which is why this
analysis is supplementary and does not feature in the paper itself. Method, results and caveats:
[`../supplement/human_baseline.md`](../supplement/human_baseline.md).

## Generation protocol

| Parameter | Value | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Topics | the four above | Improta et al. §2.1 |
| User prompts | verbatim; carried in `generate_texts.py` | Improta et al. §2.1 — checked string-for-string against the paper |
| Texts per model per topic | 1,000 | Improta et al. — their sample size, matched |
| System prompt | "Below is an instruction…" | Improta et al. §2.1 — their *local-model* pre-prompt, applied here to every model |
| Temperature | 0.5 | Improta et al. §2.1 — applied here to every model, including the APIs they excepted |
| `top_p` | 0.95 | reconstructed — the LM Studio / llama.cpp default |
| top-k, repetition / frequency / presence penalties | unset | engine defaults, as in the source run |
| `max_tokens` | 2048 | this study |

Three of those rows carry a qualification, and the qualifications matter more
than the values.

**The unstated sampling parameters are reconstructed.** Improta et
al. report only model, quantisation and temperature — no `top_p`, no top-k, no
penalties, no token cap. Those parameters still had values in their run, and
their paper says where the values came from: "All non-API models were employed
locally through LM Studio … as .GGUF files" (§2). So the settings they left
unstated were LM Studio's defaults. We reconstructed
theirs: `top_p` is set explicitly to **0.95**, the LM Studio / llama.cpp
default, and top-k and the repetition, frequency and presence penalties are left
unset so the serving engine's own defaults apply — most likely, the position their run was
in. Our pipeline does not put those four in the request payload at all.

This is an assumption: it holds only if they ran LM Studio
at its defaults. It is also the one assumption here that was tested. An
isolating run at ollama's default `top_p=0.9` against one at 0.95 differed no
more than two same-setup runs differ from each other, so `top_p` is not what
separates the cohorts. What stays unrecoverable is the engine's *version* — LM
Studio's template handling changed between 2024 and 2026 — and that residue is
the engine confound described under [the anchor](#the-mistral-7b-anchor).

**Two places where this study deliberately departs from their protocol.**
Improta et al. varied their settings by model type; this study holds them
constant instead, so that differences between corpora are attributable to the
models rather than to the harness:

- *System prompt.* They used two pre-prompts, both left at product defaults:
  "You are a helpful assistant" for ChatGPT, and "Below is an instruction that
  describes a task. Write a response that appropriately completes the request."
  for the models served through LM Studio (§2.1). This study uses the second
  string for **every** model, including the three closed APIs.
- *Temperature.* They set 0.5 for all models **except ChatGPT**, which kept its
  provider default — and that default is not even a fixed number, being set from
  log-probability thresholds (§2.1). This study sets 0.5 uniformly, APIs
  included.

Both departures make this study's own cohort internally consistent, at the cost
of not reproducing the source protocol exactly for the API models. The 2024
cohort is used as deposited, so its texts carry their settings.

**The token cap is genuinely this study's own choice** — a ceiling set well
above the observed range rather than an inherited or reconstructed setting. Note
that the source prompts themselves end "Maximise the length of the reply", so
output length is under prompt pressure in both cohorts.

Observed output is short relative to the cap — a median around 500 tokens, with
real cap hits concentrated in one model (4–16% depending on topic) and below
0.5% for every other.

Three provider behaviours are handled explicitly in `generate_texts.py`:
`--no-system-role` merges the system prompt into the user turn for servers that
enforce a chat template with no system slot; `--no-thinking` disables reasoning
output; GPT-5 and o-series models use `max_completion_tokens` instead of
`max_tokens`. Generation resumes by row count, so an interrupted run continues
rather than restarting.

## Cleaning and parsing

[`../scripts/prepare/clean_corpus.py`](../scripts/prepare/clean_corpus.py) is
the pre-parser referenced in §3 of the chapter. It deduplicates, then annotates
every text with eight boolean flags in two groups:

- **Quality** — `flag_truncation`, `flag_repetition_loop`, `flag_code_contam`,
  `flag_non_english`. These mark texts excluded from the distance analyses.
- **Style** — `flag_ai_self_ref`, `flag_disclaimer`, `flag_refusal`,
  `flag_hedge_heavy_opener`. These are *measured*: opener style
  and disclaimer rate are themselves reported findings.

Truncation is inferred rather than reported by the API: a text is flagged when
its word count reaches **0.80 × the token cap** and it ends without terminal
punctuation. Non-English detection uses `langdetect` with a fixed seed.
Repetition loops are detected by n-gram recurrence above a count-and-ratio
threshold.

Parsing is spaCy `en_core_web_lg`, serialised to DocBins by
[`python3 -m aiidiolects.parse_corpus`](../src/aiidiolects/parse_corpus.py), so
the syntactic stage reads a fixed parse rather than re-parsing.

## Measures

The fingerprint library is
[`compare_fingerprints.py`](../src/aiidiolects/compare_fingerprints.py). Parameters
are the ones actually used:

| Level | Measure | Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| Distributional | Text length, sentence length | two-sample KS test; Cohen's *d* |
| Lexical | Type–token ratio | corpus level |
| Lexical | MATTR | window = 500 tokens |
| Lexical | Hapax legomena ratio, Yule's K | — |
| Lexical | Jaccard overlap | top 100 words |
| N-gram | Bigram and trigram Jensen–Shannon divergence | top 500 n-grams |
| Stylometric | Burrows' Δ | 100 most frequent words, chunked |

Beyond the pairwise library, the analysis stages add:

- **Distance matrices and dendrograms** over all corpora, with bootstrap
  confidence intervals and MDS biplots.
- **Discourse features** — punctuation profile, discourse markers, hedges.
- **Syntactic measures** — dependency length, mean dependency distance and mean
  hierarchical distance, tree depth, passive rate, subordination, noun-chunk
  length, POS n-gram JSD, dependency-label JSD. Method follows Juzek, Krielke &
  Teich (UDW 2020).
- **Within-corpus variation** — split-half Burrows' Δ per corpus, measuring
  internal heterogeneity.
- **Keyness** — baseline-free: for each corpus, the words it over-uses relative
  to a reference pool of the others, by log-likelihood G² with Hardie's
  log-ratio effect size and a split-half consensus filter.
- **Perplexity** under a fixed external yardstick (GPT-2-large, a 2019 model),
  which measures distance from that model's expectations rather than intrinsic
  fluency.
- **Sentence-embedding map** — `intfloat/e5-large-v2` with UMAP, plus mean
  internal cosine as a semantic-redundancy measure.

The last two are GPU-oriented; [`lambda_setup.md`](lambda_setup.md) covers
running them on a cloud instance.

## The Mistral-7B anchor

Cross-cohort comparison is only meaningful if this study's generation setup
produces output comparable to the source study's. The anchor establishes that,
and bounds what remains unexplained.

Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 Q8_0 was re-generated at full scale under the source
study's prompts and temperature, served through LM Studio 0.4.11. A 
**second, independent run under identical conditions** provides the stochastic noise
floor: two same-setup runs already differ by some amount, and a raw distance
against the originals is uninterpretable without that floor. Engine continuity
between the two is deliberate — re-serving through a different engine would
reintroduce a variance the anchor exists to control.

Replication was judged against thresholds fixed in advance, calibrated on a
same-model split-half comparison:

| Measure | Equivalent | Divergent |
|---|---|---|
| Burrows' Δ | < 0.5 | > 1.0 |
| Bigram JSD | < 0.10 | > 0.20 |
| Jaccard top-100 | > 0.80 | < 0.65 |
| Length KS *p* | > 0.05 | < 0.01 |
| Length Cohen's *d* | \|d\| < 0.3 | \|d\| > 0.5 |

**Outcome.** The pipeline is internally consistent — independent same-setup runs
sit at the noise floor. The gap against the 2024 originals is systematic, several times the noise floor, and lands in the grey zone on
Burrows' Δ. It is most likely attributable to inference-environment details the source paper
does not report: engine choice, chat-template handling, and undocumented
sampling defaults, none of which are recoverable for a 2024 run. Full numbers,
including the engine-variance experiment that isolates the cause, are in
[`PHASE1_RESULTS.md`](PHASE1_RESULTS.md).

**This is a carried caveat** The 2024-cohort models other
than Mistral-7B have no 2026 re-run, so the engine confound applies to them
throughout, and it is reported as such in the chapter.
